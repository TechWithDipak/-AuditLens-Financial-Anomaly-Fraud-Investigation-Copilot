# FinCEN Advisory FIN-2020-A001: Advisory on Ransomware and Cyber-Extortion

## 1. Background and Threat Landscape
Ransomware is a form of malicious software designed to block access to a computer system or data, often by encrypting data or system servers, until the victim pays a ransom sum to the cyber threat actor. The Financial Crimes Enforcement Network (FinCEN) is issuing this advisory to provide financial institutions with information regarding the financial mechanisms, money mule networks, and virtual currency payment funnels utilized in cyber-extortion operations.

## 2. Financial and Virtual Currency Red Flag Indicators
Cybercriminals and illicit extortion syndicates rely on specialized financial funnels to convert extortion proceeds into convertible virtual currency (CVC) and obscure ownership trails:
- A customer suddenly initiates a high-value fiat currency wire transfer or credit card cash advance to a digital asset exchange or unhosted virtual currency kiosk without prior history of cryptocurrency activities.
- A corporate customer requests an urgent, out-of-band wire transfer to an unknown foreign entity or digital asset wallet provider accompanied by communications referencing cyber crisis incident response or data decryption keys.
- Funds transmitted to cryptocurrency exchange addresses associated with known high-risk tumblers, mixers, darknet marketplaces, or peer-to-peer exchanges lacking anti-money laundering controls.
- Rapid layered hops across multiple virtual currency wallets immediately following receipt of victim funds, designed to disrupt blockchain clustering heuristics.
- Structured fiat deposits into personal accounts followed immediately by transfers to digital asset brokers on behalf of third parties (money mule patterns).

## 3. OFAC Sanctions Compliance and Cyber Extortion
Financial institutions, digital currency exchanges, and incident response firms facilitating ransom payments must consider Office of Foreign Assets Control (OFAC) sanctions risk:
- Facilitating ransom payments to cyber actors with a sanctions nexus (e.g., entities or individuals designated under OFAC cyber sanctions programs or located in comprehensive sanctions jurisdictions) threatens national security and violates federal sanctions laws.
- U.S. persons are strictly prohibited from engaging in transactions, directly or indirectly, with individuals or entities on OFAC's Specially Designated Nationals and Blocked Persons List (SDN List).
- Strict liability applies to sanctions violations; entities may be held liable even if they did not know or have reason to know that the transaction involved an SDN-listed entity.

## 4. SAR Filing Instructions for Cyber Extortion
When filing a SAR related to suspected ransomware or cyber-extortion payments:
- Include the advisory key term "CYBER-FIN-2020-A001" in the SAR narrative and Field 2.
- Provide relevant cyber threat indicators, including IP addresses with timestamp and timezone, digital wallet addresses, hashes of malicious payloads, communication channels, and Bitcoin/CVC transaction hashes.
- Identify the victim entity, intermediate virtual currency service providers, and beneficial account holders where known.
