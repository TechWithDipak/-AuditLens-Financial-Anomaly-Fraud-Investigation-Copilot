# FATF Guidance: Red Flag Indicators of Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing

## 1. Executive Summary and Scope
The Financial Action Task Force (FATF) issues international standards and guidance to assist competent authorities and reporting financial entities in recognizing transactional patterns commonly associated with illicit financial flows, trade-based money laundering, and terrorist financing. Reporting entities should adopt risk-based monitoring systems that flag complex, unusually large transactions or unusual patterns of transactions that have no apparent economic or lawful purpose.

## 2. Structuring and Smurfing Indicators
Structuring involves deliberately breaking down monetary transactions into smaller increments to evade currency reporting requirements and automated surveillance thresholds. Indicators include:
- Multiple cash deposits or electronic transfers into one or more accounts conducted on the same day or over several consecutive days, each slightly beneath statutory reporting thresholds (e.g., $10,000 cash threshold).
- Deposits made at multiple branch locations, automated teller machines (ATMs), or digital payment gateways in rapid succession by the same individual or affiliated parties.
- A sudden surge in transaction frequency where funds are accumulated in micro-deposits and then rapidly aggregated into a single high-value outflow.
- Customer inquiries specifically regarding mandatory reporting limits or requests to cancel transactions upon learning of identity verification or declaration obligations.

## 3. Account Utilization and Velocity Anomalies
Anomalies in account velocity and fund flow directionality often signal illicit pass-through operations:
- A previously dormant or low-activity account abruptly experiences intense transaction turnover involving high aggregate volumes inconsistent with the customer's declared business or personal profile.
- Rapid movement of funds into an account followed almost immediately by complete disbursement to unrelated entities, offshore jurisdictions, or high-risk third parties (pass-through / transit account topology).
- Substantial round-trip transactions where money is transferred to an external jurisdiction and subsequently returned within a short interval with no discernible economic rationale.
- Frequent high-value transfers between entities sharing common beneficial owners or registered addresses without corresponding commercial rationale.

## 4. Complex and Unconventional Fund Movement
Sophisticated laundering networks routinely use layered corporate entities, trade invoicing discrepancies, and non-bank financial intermediaries:
- Wire transfers directed toward or originating from jurisdictions identified by the FATF as High-Risk Jurisdictions subject to a Call for Action or Jurisdictions under Increased Monitoring (FATF Grey/Black Lists).
- Excessive reliance on third-party payment processors or money service businesses (MSBs) to obscure the ultimate originator or beneficiary.
- Discrepancies between the stated nature of commercial business and transactional counterparts (e.g., a software development firm transacting heavily with maritime logistics brokers in offshore tax havens).
