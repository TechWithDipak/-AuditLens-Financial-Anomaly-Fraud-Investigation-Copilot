# FFIEC BSA/AML Examination Manual: Suspicious Activity Reporting (SAR)

## 1. Statutory Mandate and Filing Thresholds
Under the Bank Secrecy Act (BSA) regulations (12 CFR 21.11, 12 CFR 208.62, 12 CFR 353.3, 12 CFR 390.355, and 31 CFR 1020.320), banking organizations are required to file Suspicious Activity Reports (SARs) regarding known or suspected violations of federal law or suspicious transactions related to money laundering or violations of the BSA.
Filing thresholds include:
- Insider abuse involving any dollar amount.
- Transactions aggregating $5,000 or more that involve potential money laundering or violations of the BSA where a suspect can be identified.
- Transactions aggregating $25,000 or more that involve potential money laundering or violations of the BSA regardless of whether a potential suspect can be identified.
- Transactions aggregating $5,000 or more that have no business or apparent lawful purpose or are not the sort in which the particular customer would normally be expected to engage.

## 2. Regulatory Timing and Submission Deadlines
Compliance officers and investigators must strictly adhere to statutory filing deadlines:
- The SAR must be filed no later than 30 calendar days after the date of initial detection by the banking organization of facts that may constitute a basis for filing.
- If no suspect was identified on the date of initial detection of the incident, the institution may delay filing for an additional 30 calendar days to attempt suspect identification, but in no case may filing be delayed beyond 60 calendar days after initial detection.
- Ongoing suspicious activity requires continuing activity SAR filings every 90 calendar days to capture recurring transaction patterns.
- Situations involving ongoing violations requiring immediate law enforcement attention (such as active terrorist financing or ongoing cyber intrusion) require immediate notification to law enforcement by telephone in addition to timely SAR filing.

## 3. Strict Prohibition Against Tipping Off
Federal law strictly prohibits disclosing the existence or content of a SAR or any information that would reveal whether a SAR has been prepared or filed:
- Under 31 U.S.C. 5318(g)(2) and implementing regulations, no bank, officer, employee, or agent of a bank shall notify any person involved in the transaction that the transaction has been reported.
- Internal investigation case files, communications with internal legal counsel, and analyst working notes reflecting SAR deliberation must remain strictly confidential and shielded from third-party discovery under the statutory SAR privilege.
- Violations of the non-disclosure prohibition carry civil and criminal penalties for the institution and the individual employee.

## 4. Documentation Retention and Audit Trail Standards
A financial institution must maintain copies of all SARs filed and supporting documentation for a period of five (5) years from the date of filing:
- Supporting documentation includes account ledgers, transaction records, canceled checks, wire transfer confirmations, customer due diligence files, and internal investigator worksheets.
- Supporting documentation is deemed to be filed with the SAR and must be made readily available to FinCEN and appropriate law enforcement agencies upon lawful request without the need for a subpoena.
