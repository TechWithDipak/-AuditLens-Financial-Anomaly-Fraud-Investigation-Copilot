**Publication Date:** 2014-10-27

## Section 1: Core Principles of the Risk-Based Approach
The risk-based approach (RBA) is central to the effective implementation of the FATF Recommendations. Under the RBA, competent authorities and financial institutions are expected to identify, assess, and understand the money laundering and terrorist financing (ML/TF) risks to which they are exposed and take AML/CFT measures commensurate to those risks in order to mitigate them effectively.

Banks should identify and assess ML/TF risks associated with customers, countries or geographic areas, products, services, transactions, and delivery channels.

---

## Section 2: Customer Due Diligence (CDD) and Enhanced Due Diligence (EDD)
Financial institutions must apply Customer Due Diligence (CDD) measures when:
1. Establishing business relations.
2. Carrying out occasional transactions above the designated threshold (EUR/USD 15,000) or wire transfers above EUR/USD 1,000.
3. There is a suspicion of money laundering or terrorist financing, regardless of any threshold.
4. There are doubts about the veracity or adequacy of previously obtained customer identification data.

Where the ML/TF risks are higher, banks must conduct Enhanced Due Diligence (EDD) measures consistent with the risks identified. Higher risk indicators include:
- Unusually complex, large transactions, or unusual patterns of transactions that have no apparent economic or visible lawful purpose.
- Non-resident customers or customers from higher-risk jurisdictions lacking adequate AML/CFT regimes.
- Use of corporate vehicles, shell companies, or complex legal arrangements that obscure the ultimate beneficial ownership (UBO).

## Section 3: Ongoing Monitoring and Transaction Scrutiny
Ongoing monitoring is an essential component of the risk-based approach. Banks should maintain current information and scrutinize transactions undertaken throughout the course of that relationship to ensure that transactions are consistent with the institution's knowledge of the customer, their business and risk profile, including the source of funds where necessary.

Key monitoring indicators:
- Sudden changes in transaction velocity or transaction amounts inconsistent with the customer's known historical baseline.
- High velocity of funds entering an account followed immediately by outgoing transfers to unrelated third parties (pass-through or funnel account behavior).
- Transaction structuring designed to evade regulatory reporting thresholds.
- Multiple transactions conducted on the same day or over a short sequence of days just below mandatory identification thresholds.
