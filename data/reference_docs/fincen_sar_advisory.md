# FinCEN Advisory on Structuring and Suspicious Activity Reporting (SAR)

**Issuing Body:** Financial Crimes Enforcement Network (FinCEN)  
**Publication Date:** 2021-03-12  
**Version:** 2.1  
**Source URL:** https://www.fincen.gov/resources/advisories  
**Permitted Use:** Public guidance and informational advisory.

---

## Section 1: Statutory Obligations and SAR Filing Mandates
Under the Bank Secrecy Act (BSA) and its implementing regulations (31 CFR § 1020.320), financial institutions are required to report suspicious transactions conducted or attempted by, at, or through a bank.

A transaction requires filing a Suspicious Activity Report (SAR) if it involves or aggregates at least $5,000 in funds or other assets, and the institution knows, suspects, or has reason to suspect that:
1. The transaction involves funds derived from illegal activities or is intended to hide or disguise funds from illicit origins.
2. The transaction is designed, whether through structuring or other means, to evade BSA requirements, including Currency Transaction Report (CTR) filing thresholds ($10,000).
3. The transaction has no business or apparent lawful purpose or is not the sort in which the particular customer would normally be expected to engage.

## Section 2: Red Flags for Structuring and Smurfing
Structuring occurs when an individual conducts or attempts to conduct one or more transactions in currency, in any amount, at one or more financial institutions, on one or more days, in any manner, for the purpose of evading currency transaction reporting requirements.

Key behavioral and quantitative structuring red flags:
- Multiple cash deposits or transfers conducted just below the $10,000 threshold (e.g., $9,500, $9,800, or $9,950).
- Deposits into multiple accounts controlled by related entities within a short temporal window followed by rapid consolidation wire transfers.
- Inconsistent transaction velocity: A sudden surge in transaction frequency accompanied by immediate funds dissipation.
- Customer inquiries specifically probing reporting thresholds or attempting to cancel or reduce a transaction upon being informed that identification is required.

---

## Section 3: Mule Accounts and Digital Layering Schemes
FinCEN emphasizes the identification of money mule networks and unauthorized account takeovers:
- New accounts that immediately receive rapid electronic fund transfers or peer-to-peer (P2P) payments from multiple distinct senders, followed by immediate ATM cash withdrawals or crypto purchases.
- Multiple unlinked customer accounts accessing online banking services from the identical IP address, device fingerprint, or MAC address.
- Geographic dispersion anomalies: Transactions executed sequentially across geographically improbable locations within unrealistic transit time windows.
